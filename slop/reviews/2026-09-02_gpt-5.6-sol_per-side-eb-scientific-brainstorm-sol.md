# 1. Causal story and its assumptions

## Observations

1. The target cotangent is one hidden-state mean contrast:
   \[
   c=\bar h_T(P_+)-\bar h_T(P_-).
   \]
2. Each prompt supplies a VJP \(J_i^\top c\), evaluated at that prompt’s own trajectory.
3. Positive and negative prompts are averaged and shrunk separately.
4. Side-specific activation standard deviations enter twice in the numerator and once through global normalization:
   \[
   d_{s,l}=\frac{\sigma_{s,l}^2r_{s,l}}
   {\sqrt{\sum_k\|\sigma_{s,k}r_{s,k}\|^2}}.
   \]
5. The vectors are then applied with opposite signs.
6. EB and the previous estimator have cosines around 0.86–0.89, so they are related but materially different directions.
7. The accepted measured regions differ in dose, effect, and damage; the original absence of an accepted \(-C\) point was due to inadequate low-dose coverage.

## Inferred intended causal story

The apparent intended story is:

1. The target contrast \(c\) encodes the desired positive-versus-negative behavioral trait.
2. \(J_i^\top c\) identifies source-layer perturbations that locally increase the target representation’s projection onto that contrast for prompt \(i\).
3. Averaging within a class extracts a stable class-conditioned causal route.
4. EB suppresses coordinates whose observed mean VJP is mostly sampling noise.
5. Activation scaling converts source coordinates into behaviorally comparable units.
6. Applying the positive-conditioned route positively and the negative-conditioned route negatively should move behavior toward opposite ends of the same semantic dimension.

Only item 2 follows locally from the VJP: for an infinitesimal source perturbation \(\delta x\),
\[
c^\top\delta h_T \approx (J_i^\top c)^\top\delta x.
\]
Even this guarantees movement only in the scalar hidden-state readout \(c^\top h_T\), not in the judged behavioral trait.

## Strong assumptions—not consequences of VJP or EB

### A. The mean hidden-state contrast is the desired trait

**Inference:** \(c\) may mix sycophancy/abrasiveness with sentiment, politeness, refusal, lexical format, length, topic, or prompt-template differences.

A VJP faithfully optimizes whatever is in \(c\); it does not validate the semantics of \(c\).

### B. A local derivative predicts finite-dose behavior

The accepted doses are finite interventions in a deep nonlinear network. Jacobian rotation, gating changes, residual-stream feedback, layer normalization, attention redistribution, and generation-state changes can invalidate the first-order approximation.

### C. Class-conditioned VJP means estimate the same latent object

\[
E[J^\top c\mid P_+]\quad\text{and}\quad E[J^\top c\mid P_-]
\]
need not be positive and negative versions of one axis. They are derivatives at different regions of model state space. The model may use different circuits on the two prompt classes.

### D. Negating the negative-class vector has the intended semantics

The negative VJP itself points locally toward increasing \(c^\top h_T\) on negative prompts. Applying its negative points toward decreasing that scalar locally. It does not follow that this produces “abrasiveness,” nor that it is the semantic inverse of applying the positive vector positively.

This sign issue should be verified against the exact application convention and first-order predicted change.

### E. EB is “noise removal”

The EB rule assumes, approximately, exchangeable coordinate effects within each layer under a zero-centered normal prior with one layer-level signal variance. Coordinates of a transformer VJP are correlated, heavy-tailed, structured by neurons/channels, and not generally exchangeable.

Moreover, EB does not merely reduce variance. Compared with the prior estimator, it changes:

- coordinate sparsity,
- relative coordinate amplitudes,
- layer allocation after normalization,
- sensitivity to dense weak signals versus sparse strong signals.

### F. Activation standard deviations define the correct intervention metric

Side-specific \(\sigma\) is a preconditioner, not an innocuous unit conversion. Because it changes both direction and normalization, it defines which coordinates and layers are considered cheap or important. Distinct side-specific \(\sigma\) values mean the two vectors are constructed under different metrics.

### G. Prompt samples are effectively independent and pairing is irrelevant

The prompts are paired, but the variance model treats each side independently. If pair-level topic, structure, or length creates correlated VJP variation, ignoring pairing discards useful cancellation and can misestimate uncertainty for the intended contrast.

# 2. Why the two sides can legitimately have different curves

Below, “legitimately” means the asymmetry can arise from the construction and model without implying an implementation failure.

## Mechanism 1: Nonlinear geometry and Jacobian rotation

**Speculation:** The positive and negative prompts occupy different activation regions. Their Jacobians can differ in direction, singular spectrum, and curvature. A perturbation may remain locally aligned over a large interval on one side but rapidly rotate into off-target behavior on the other.

This can produce:

- different onset thresholds,
- different slopes,
- saturation,
- sign reversals,
- abrupt damage cliffs.

**Supporting observation:** Hidden-state projection onto \(c\) initially changes as predicted but bends or saturates at higher doses; layer activations or logits show abrupt transitions near the damage threshold.

**Economical test:** On held-out prompts, measure both the realized \(\Delta(c^\top h_T)\) and behavioral effect over a dense low-to-moderate dose grid. Compare them with the first-order prediction \(g^\top\delta\). Early divergence supports nonlinear geometry; behavioral divergence despite accurate hidden-state prediction instead implicates semantic mismatch.

## Mechanism 2: Class-conditioned Jacobians use different circuits

**Speculation:** The model may produce agreeable behavior through one set of features and abrasive behavior through another. Then
\[
E[J^\top c\mid +]\not\parallel E[J^\top c\mid -].
\]
Separate extraction can correctly discover distinct routes, even if the endpoint contrast is shared.

**Supporting observation:** Low cosine between raw side means or different per-layer energy profiles, with each vector having its strongest first-order and behavioral effect on its own conditioning class.

**Economical test:** Cross the vectors and evaluation classes: evaluate each of `±d["+C"]` and `±d["-C"]` on held-out positive, negative, and neutral prompts. Class-specific effectiveness supports distinct circuits. Broad sign-consistent effects support a shared direction.

## Mechanism 3: Prompt-distribution and token-length differences

**Speculation:** Differences in token count, padding, truncation, instruction structure, or where persona-defining information occurs can alter VJPs. Longer prompts may aggregate more token contributions or change which positions survive `skip_first` and `max_length`. Topic and lexical differences can also dominate class means.

**Supporting observation:** VJP norm, layer allocation, or vector projection is predictable from token length/template/topic within a side.

**Economical test:** Recompute descriptive statistics after matching or reweighting prompts by token length and template/topic, without changing model extraction. Check whether side mean VJPs and \(\sigma\)-weighted layer energy materially shift. A major shift indicates distributional rather than trait asymmetry.

## Mechanism 4: Side-specific activation normalization changes the metric

**Speculation:** \(\sigma_s^2\) amplifies high-variance coordinates, while the denominator normalizes in a related but not identical standardized norm. Consequently the same raw gradient structure can become different directions and have different effective activation-space doses across sides.

This is more than calibration. In general,
\[
d_+\neq d_-
\]
even if the raw \(r_+\) and \(r_-\) were similar, because \(\sigma_+\neq\sigma_-\).

**Supporting observation:** Much of the side-vector angle or layer-energy difference disappears when comparing raw \(r_s\), or when both sides are expressed under a common pooled \(\sigma\).

**Economical test:** Offline, with no new model runs, compare side cosine and layer energy at four stages: raw means, EB-shrunk \(r\), common-\(\sigma\) preconditioning, and side-\(\sigma\) preconditioning. This localizes whether asymmetry comes from the VJPs, EB, or the metric.

## Mechanism 5: Different estimator SNR and EB behavior

**Speculation:** One side may have a dense, repeatable mean gradient and the other a heterogeneous or sparse gradient. The layer-level EB prior favors dense layer-wide signal differently from coordinatewise positive-part shrinkage. If \(\tau_l^2\) is estimated near zero, an entire layer is effectively suppressed; if a few large coordinates inflate it, many weak coordinates receive nontrivial weight.

**Supporting observation:** The sides differ in split-half stability, \(\tau_l^2\), effective number of retained coordinates, or concentration of layer energy. EB improves stability more on the side with distributed moderate-SNR signal.

**Economical test:** Bootstrap paired prompts and report vector angular stability plus layer-energy uncertainty for raw mean, old shrinker, and EB. Stability improvement without behavioral improvement means EB estimated the sample mean more reproducibly, not necessarily the trait better.

**Speculation:** The evaluator-relevant trait may be expressed only after a latent threshold. # 4. Ranked discriminating tests

## Rank 1 — Crossed vector/sign/class dose-response

Evaluate:

- \(+d_+\),
- \(-d_+\),
- \(+d_-\),
- \(-d_-\),

on held-out positive, negative, and neutral prompts, with a common dense standardized-dose grid and the same evaluators.

### Exact discrimination

- **One shared bidirectional axis:** both vectors should have broadly similar sign-reversed effects; class dependence should be limited.
- **Distinct class-conditioned circuits:** each vector should work best on its own conditioning distribution.
- **Generic valence/agreement:** effects should transfer broadly to neutral tasks and load on agreement/politeness.
- **Sign or cotangent mismatch:** an allegedly intended sign may move \(c^\top h_T\) or behavior in the opposite direction.

This is the single most informative behavioral test because the current design otherwise confounds extraction side with application sign.

---

## Rank 2 — First-order mediation and curvature check

At each dose, measure:

1. predicted \(\Delta(c^\top h_T)\) from the VJP,
2. realized \(\Delta(c^\top h_T)\),

4. damage.

- Predicted ≈ realized, but behavior mismatches: **\(c\) is semantically inadequate**.

# 5. Paired and joint/two-sided alternatives

## Paired estimator

For matched pair \(i\), define
\[
\Delta g_i = g_i^+ - g_i^-,
\]
then estimate and shrink \(E[\Delta g_i]\), preferably using pair-level resampling.

### Exact hypothesis tested

> There is a source perturbation whose local effect on the shared target contrast differs systematically between matched positive and negative contexts after canceling pair-shared content and template variation.

Depending on the desired semantics, one might instead test a paired sum:
\[
g_i^+ + g_i^-,
\]
which asks whether the same perturbation increases \(c^\top h_T\) in both contexts.

### Potential benefit

- Cancels pair-shared topic and lexical effects.
- Uses covariance between pair members.
- Estimates uncertainty for the contrast actually induced by persona condition.
- Can yield a more identifiable “difference in routing” object.

### Potential confound

- If the desired intervention should work similarly on both classes, differencing can cancel the common useful route.
- Pair quality becomes critical.
- Differences in length or syntax within pairs can masquerade as persona-conditioned routing.
- A paired difference identifies **differential sensitivity**, not necessarily a universal trait direction.

---

## Joint/two-sided extraction

One possible joint objective is to find a single source vector \(v\) maximizing average target movement across both classes:
\[
\max_{\|v\|_M=1}
\left[
E(g^+)^\top v + E(g^-)^\top v
\right].
\]

A contrastive alternative maximizes separation:
\[
\max_{\|v\|_M=1}
\left[
E(g^+)^\top v - E(g^-)^\top v
\right].
\]

A stronger bidirectionality criterion could maximize the minimum effect:
\[
\max_{\|v\|_M=1}\min\{E(g^+)^\top v,\ E(g^-)^\top v\}.
\]

### Exact hypotheses

- **Joint-sum:** one source direction moves the target contrast consistently in both prompt regions.
- **Joint-difference:** the scientific object is differential routing between classes.
- **Max-min:** a universal direction exists that is robust across both classes.

### Potential benefit

- Produces one metric and one axis, making \(+C\) and \(-C\) more interpretable.
- Prevents each side from selecting unrelated circuitry.
- Directly tests whether a common route exists.
- Makes opposite-sign applications a coherent bidirectional experiment.

### Potential confounds

- A common objective can average away legitimate class-specific mechanisms.
- The higher-SNR or larger-gradient class can dominate unless balanced.
- Joint normalization may privilege one activation distribution.
- A symmetric axis can perform worse behaviorally if the model truly implements the behaviors through different circuits.

Thus paired and joint estimators answer different questions. Neither is unconditionally “better”; the choice should follow the intended estimand.
