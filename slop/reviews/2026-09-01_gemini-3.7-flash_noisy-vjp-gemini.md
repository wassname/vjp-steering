### Estimator 1: Empirical Bayes Gaussian Shrinkage (James-Stein / Normal-Normal Model)

1. **Model / Objective (Derivation):**
   Assume true coordinate effect $\theta_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2)$ with noisy observation $\hat{\mu}_i \mid \theta_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\theta_i, s_i^2)$ where sample variance of the mean is $s_i^2 = v_i / n$. 
   The posterior mean is:
   $$\mathbb{E}[\theta_i \mid \hat{\mu}_i] = \hat{\mu}_i \cdot \frac{\tau^2}{\tau^2 + s_i^2} = \hat{\mu}_i \cdot \left(1 - \frac{v_i / n}{\tau^2 + v_i / n}\right)$$
   With common prior variance $\tau^2 = \max\left(0, \frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^d (\mu_j^2 - v_j/n)\right)$.

2. **Pseudocode:**
   ```python
   # Empirical Bayes posterior mean
   sem_sq = v / n
   tau_sq = maximum(0.0, mean(μ**2 - sem_sq))
   w = tau_sq / (tau_sq + sem_sq)
   r = μ * w
   ```

3. **Role of $n$:**
   $n$ **must** enter because shrinkage operates on the *sampling variance of the estimator* ($\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\mu}) = v/n$), not on the population variance of the prompt distribution ($v$). Shrinkage should vanish as $n \to \infty$; omitting $n$ (as the baseline code does with $v/\mu^2$) incorrectly penalizes true cross-prompt heterogeneity even under infinite sample size.

4. **Interaction with Steepest-Ascent Constraint:**
   The Mahalanobis constraint $\|d / \sigma\|_2 = 1$ transforms the coordinate metric from activation units to standardized isotropic units. Applying EB shrinkage to $\mu$ finds the $L_2$-optimal linear predictor of the gradient $r \approx \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$, preserving gradient orientation before the transformation $d = \sigma^2 r / \|\sigma r\|_2$ maps it onto the activation ellipsoid.

5. **Failure Mode:**
   If a small subset of persona prompts triggers extreme outlier gradients in a few coordinates, $v_i$ explodes locally while $\mu_i$ remains modest.
