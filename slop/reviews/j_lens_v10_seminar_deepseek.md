## Connections, objections, extensions, different framings

### Connections between the two scientist reviews

Both reviews converge on the same core reconstruction: v16 is a two-component, full-residual, per-position coordinate-exchange operator, not an additive persona steer and not the corrected J-lens readout. The GLM and Kimi reviews both flag the `max/min` clamp as a **rank-ordering / exchange operation** rather than directed addition, and both identify the replay as implementation-fidelity evidence only. They independently make the same key distinction: numerical replay passes establish the patch math is correct, but say nothing about whether the extracted directions encode sycophancy.

The strongest shared inference is the **basis contamination hypothesis**: full-residual difference-in-means vectors necessarily include topic, length, tone, and register differences between persona conditions, and the attached evidence provides no measurement that the vectors isolate sycophancy. Both reviews name this as the leading explanation for weak transfer, and both propose the same diagnostic—decode top unembedding projections or measure cosine similarity to topic/length nuisance directions.

A second shared point: the per-position independence assumption is untested. Both note that swapping coordinates independently at every position can produce an internally inconsistent state, and neither author can find evidence that the natural representation exhibits the local ordering the patch imposes.

A third shared empirical observation: the aggregate is fragile. The minus side has median 0 and only 6/15 intended signs, yet the mean is dragged negative by at least one large-magnitude example (`sw_pnf_02`, −7.7). Both call for per-question distributions and bootstrap intervals before interpreting means.

### Where the reviews differ

**Framing of the plus/minus asymmetry.** GLM frames it primarily as the negative persona being a weaker or noisier direction at extraction time. Kimi emphasizes the mathematical asymmetry of the operating point: because the operator is conditional on the observed order, the two sides do not apply the same intervention unless the natural distribution of coordinate order is symmetric.

**Depth on the non-orthogonal basis.** Kimi goes further on the semantic ambiguity of a 2D dual readout with non-orthogonal rows. GLM notes the condition numbers are fine but treats that as sufficient for well-conditioned clamping. Kimi correctly points out that well-conditioned does not mean semantically separable: two nearly orthogonal directions could still encode style and content, not two ends of one construct. Kimi proposes concrete metrics—`ρ_ℓ` (cosine similarity between the two basis rows) and the projection residual fraction `r_ℓ(h)`—that would resolve this.

**The dose-monotonicity explanation.** Both note non-monotonicity, but Kimi gives a sharper mechanism: the intervention norm is state-dependent, scaling as approximately `|α||c₁−c₀||b₀−b₁|` when an exchange occurs. So nominal alpha is not a controlled dose; two prompts with the same alpha can receive very different perturbation magnitudes. This is the strongest mechanistic thread in either review. GLM attributes non-monotonicity more vaguely to off-manifold saturation, which is weaker because it doesn't explain why some alphas would show non-monotonicity at low doses where saturation seems less plausible.

### Objections to the existing reviews

**Kimi's repeated call for random steering controls omits what is already pinned.** The historical selection rule explicitly requires comparison against random steering, and the evidence packet states the desired deliverable is "compared with random steering." So the absence of random steering is a missing *attachment*, not necessarily a missing *experiment*. Kimi and GLM both phrase it as though the experiment may not have been run, which overstates the gap. A fairer objection is: the random steering results are not in this packet, so the reader cannot assess whether weak transfer is above the null, and the historical selection rule's status is unverifiable from here.

**Both reviews treat the direct instruction control as evidence of what the rubric can detect.** This is sound in direction but they under-weight the asymmetry. The −6.37 result is from a different pipeline, from an aggregate only, and with raw outputs unverified in this packet. GLM and Kimi both use it, but Kimi correctly incorporates the caveat that it cannot be audited. GLM uses it more freely as an expected ceiling. I would not lean on it beyond: a judge can produce a large negative mean when the intervention is conceptually direct.

**Kimi's claim that "alpha 1 is the exact exchange reference point" needs refinement.** In a non-orthogonal basis, setting `desired = (high, low)` and adding `(desired − c)B` indeed exactly realizes the target coordinates at alpha 1 in the *contravariant readout*. But because the basis rows are not orthogonal, exact equality in dual coordinates is not exact equality in hidden-state geometry; it does not mean the patch "only" swaps orderings. The operational meaning of alpha 1 is coordinate-exact, not representation-exact. Kimi gestures at this with the caveat about nonorthogonality but then repeatedly falls back on describing it as exact exchange. Minor, but it matters for interpreting where overshoot begins.

**GLM's alternative list includes "difference-of-personas direction (s₊ − s₋) with a single signed coordinate" as an untested classical delta method.** This is good, but it should be paired with a critical question: if s₊ and s₋ are meant to be two ends of one construct, why construct the 2D clamp at all instead of testing the single signed direction first? The burden is on the 2D construction to show added value; neither review states this inversion of the default clearly enough.

### Extensions not fully developed in either review

**The min-coordinate may not be doing what the authors think.** Both reviews mention the min-coordinate pathology in passing. But there is a sharper observation: the plus operator sets the second coordinate to `low`, meaning it actively *pushes down* the negative-component coordinate whenever it is above the positive one. If the negative component partially encodes critical resistance to user framing, then the plus side is doing more than boosting positivity—it is suppressing whatever residual negativity was present. The minus side does the mirror. This means each side implicates the other component in a way that additive steering would not. The only clean test is the asymmetric clamp (steer one coordinate, leave the other untouched), which both reviews list but neither flags as the single most diagnostically informative ablation.

**The intervention is applied at every layer 13–21.** Every layer uses basis vectors extracted without regard to the perturbations injected earlier in the same prefill. This is not merely a possible nonlinear interaction; it is a **compositional inconsistency**: the layer-15 basis was extracted from unperturbed states, but at inference it reads states already modified by the layer-13 patch. The forward hook is one-pass and the patch is simultaneous in code, but forward computation is feedforward, so later layers genuinely see patched earlier-layer activations. This is a structural mismatch between extraction-time and intervention-time distributions. Neither review states this as sharply as it deserves. The practical consequence: even if the bases are perfectly linear encodings, the composition of nine sequential exchanges has not been shown to retain the intended coordinate semantics at later layers. The leave-one-layer-out and recompute-after-patch proposals in Kimi's Experiment C are on the right track but don't make the extraction/inference distributional mismatch explicit.

**The judge's off-axis rubric gives no ground truth for what the patch should do.** The effect metric (on-axis) and the confound metric (off-axis) are produced by the same LLM judge against the same rubric. There is no independent behavioral target, and the rubric phrases the flaw as "known flaw in the question, established independently"—where the "independently" is asserted in the prompt, not independently verified in the pipeline. This is a circularity risk: the judge is being instructed that a prospectively fabricated premise is a flaw, which pre-decides whether resisting the user framing counts as the target behavior. Since the entire attached DEV15 subset appears to consist of fabricated scenarios, the "sycophancy" being measured is heavily influenced by how the judge prompt frames fabrication. That's not a defect in the rubric as written for its intended scope; but it means the measured effect is highly sensitive to the assumption that all questions were fabricated in the same way and that the judge reliably detects fabrication. Neither review makes this point. The two example responses are more consistent with "steered response became terser" than with "steered response resisted a fabricated premise," and the rubric may render both of these as off-axis or on-axis depending on wording.

**Position-masked intervention may be introducing a degenerate signal.** The patch applies only to attended prefill positions. In a chat format with a thinking prefill, these positions include both the user instruction and the model's own draft. If the two persona components are fit on states drawn from the model's own agentic context, then the semantic content of the direction may be localized to the model's own draft positions, not to user instruction tokens. Patching user-token positions with a direction learned from model-generated text could be a mapping error. No evidence in the packet separates position types in the steering result.

### Different framings

**Reframe the core question as "was the operator ever validated as a causal operator at all?"** The entire v16 approach rescues no causal identification. The difference-in-means extraction is associative; the patch is causal; but the link between the two—that the associative direction supports a causal coordinate—is the untested inference. Calling the basis a "lens" does not make it causal. The corrected J-lens readout, which at least applies transport and head geometry, has its own separate null result on token rank. The packet's careful distinction between the two is correct, but the implication neither review draws fully is: **the central scientific conclusion of the packet is not "sycophancy steering is weak" but "we do not know whether associative contrast directions support causal semantic intervention in this model."** That's a broader null than the reviews acknowledge.

**Reframe dose-monotonicity as a judge-dynamics problem, not a representation problem.** Suppose the judge's effect scale is nonlinear: small behavioral changes near neutral produce small scores, but crossing a wording threshold produces a jump, and further pushes produce off-axis damage that reduces the on-axis score. Then nonmonotonicity in alpha is expected even for a perfectly linear representation effect. The packet offers no independent confirmation that the rubric should be monotone in hidden-state magnitude. GLM flags this as assumption (I); Kimi frames nonmonotonicity primarily as intervention-norm heterogeneity. Both can be true, but the judge-dynamics possibility is independent and relatively cheap to test with rubric repeatability.

**Reframe the title "full-residual control" itself.** This is not a control in the "random steering" sense; it is a control *condition* for the J-lens method, using a different basis from the same prompt contrasts. There is no attached v16-vs-random comparison, so the "control" is unanchored. One could argue the packet has not yet established that v16 is even a valid negative control, because its weak transfer may reflect the same basis-selection pathology that would afflict the J-lens method itself, not the specificity of J-lens. If both methods use persona-contrast prompts and share the contamination, then v16's weakness would not discriminate J-lens validity. The packet's own statement—"this full-residual control does not itself establish a J-lens result"—is too generous in one direction: even as a control, v16's evidential weight depends on ruling out shared contamination, which has not been done.
