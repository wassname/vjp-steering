## What the pseudocode does

The setup builds a "J-lens": a Jacobian-based per-token direction set `Dℓ[t] = unembed[t] @ Jℓ`, computed once over 1000 pretraining prompts. Everything downstream expresses interventions in this lens.

**Candidate A (fixed_pair_diagnostic):** a diagnostic-only swap between the lens rows of the tokens `' abrasive'` and `' flattering'`, at layers 13–21, during prefill, then greedy decoding without hooks. It tests whether one fixed pair is viable before trying per-prompt selection.

**Candidate B (active_assessment_swap):** mirrors the paper's active-source condition. From a fixed (pre-DEV) lexicon of 7 antonym pairs (false/true, incorrect/correct, …), pick the source token with the highest clean lens activity at the assistant prefill, take its paired counterpart as target, swap their lens coordinates. It has an explicit no-op if the source activity is too weak, and an asserted floor on target activity.

**Candidate C (concept injection + controls):** builds J-space concept components for "sycophancy" and "abrasiveness" per the paper: residual to "Tell me about {concept}" at assistant prefill, mean-subtracted over 100 baselines, then a k=16 non-negative gradient pursuit over unit-normalized lens rows. Candidate C injects `α * unit(j)` at user-turn positions. Matched controls (J vs non-J vs random, all magnitude-matched) are provided to test whether any effect is J-space-specific or just generic concept steering.

**Acceptance contract:** a method must show the intended sign on both +C and −C sides, clear the random-direction zone, and keep raw answers responsive. Explicitly, coherence ≠ success.
