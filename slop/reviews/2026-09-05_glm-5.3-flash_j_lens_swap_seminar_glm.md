## 2. Genuine disagreements or distinctions one report caught and the others missed

### Kimi-only: the paper's own prose contradicts its formula

Kimi's central insight, absent from the other two reports: the paper's verbal description — "we subtract the projection onto the Soccer lens vector and add an equal-magnitude projection onto the Rugby lens vector" — is Candidate C, not Candidate A/B. The formula in the same paper describes a symmetric pseudoinverse exchange (A/B). Kimi states: "the paper's own text says... exactly what the working Qwen replication implements." Neither GLM nor Deepseek noticed this prose-formula tension explicitly. GLM noted C differs from the formula but treated A as "paper-native" without questioning whether the paper contradicts itself. Deepseek gave the rigorous algebraic proof that B−C = c_t(d_s−d_t) but also did not flag the prose. The diagnostic vindicates Kimi: the prose-described operator (C) is the one that works.

### Kimi-only: antonym-specific ill-conditioning argument

Kimi argues that antonym adjective pairs like abrasive/flattering are precisely where near-parallel or near-antiparallel lens vectors are plausible (both encode "strong sentiment adjective"), making the 2×2 Gram matrix near-singular and the dual-coordinate readout explosive. Country names may be better conditioned. GLM mentioned non-orthogonality generally but did not make the antonym-specific connection. The diagnostic's cosine measurement (0.34–0.64 for country pairs) confirms country pairs are well-conditioned but does not test the adjective pair's geometry.

### Deepseek-only: the QR-orthonormalized swap as a fourth candidate

Deepseek proposes swapping coordinates in an orthonormal basis of span{v_s, v_t} (via QR decomposition) as a theoretically motivated middle ground: it avoids raw-column scale amplification (A's problem) while still performing a true two-coordinate exchange (unlike C's one-directional transfer). This operator is untested by the diagnostic and absent from the other two reports.

### Deepseek-only: rank-1 vs rank-2 as a structural discriminator

Deepseek notes that C's patch h'−h = c_s(d_t−d_s) is rank 1, while A/B's patch is generally rank 2. This is a sharp test that could be run on captured activations without any behavioral evaluation, distinguishing which operator produced a given perturbation. Neither other report highlights this as a diagnostic tool.

### GLM: the "surface co-occurrence" framing of adjective lens vectors

GLM's sharpest sentence: "a token like `abrasive` may have a J-lens vector dominated by surface co-occurrence, not a verbalizable concept axis." This is a more specific version of the token-vs-concept concern than the other two reports offer. It predicts that even a perfectly implemented C on the abrasive/flattering pair might fail because the lens vectors don't carry behavioral semantics — a hypothesis the diagnostic does not test.

### Deepseek: explicit metric-sign audit

Deepseek proposes auditing the "effect" metric's sign convention against hand-labeled generations before trusting any α sweep. Kimi lists this as F7 (lowest-ranked failure mode) but doesn't develop it. GLM doesn't mention it. The old run's "wrong-sign at α=1" could partly be a scoring bug rather than a geometric phenomenon.

## 3. Claims weakened or falsified by the diagnostic

The diagnostic shows: on two baseline-correct country pairs, three bands, α=1, prompt-only hooks firing exactly once per layer — A and B produce 0/6 target redirects (6/6 stayed at source), while C produces 6/6 target redirects (0/6 stayed). Cosines range 0.34–0.64 (median 0.49), so the country-pair geometry is well-conditioned. Post-coordinate swap error ≤ 5e-6.

**Falsified:**

- **GLM's conditional prediction: "If A succeeds → the current sycophancy failure is concept/protocol, not operator."** A does not succeed on country pairs. The operator itself is wrong. GLM's other branch ("if A fails and C succeeds → the difference is operator geometry") is confirmed.
- **Deepseek's inference that the paper's stated operator is not the operative mechanism** — confirmed by the diagnostic, not falsified. Deepseek explicitly predicted: "If candidate A fails but C succeeds under same conditions, then paper's stated operator is not the operative mechanism." That is what happened.
- **The implicit assumption in all three reports that A is "the paper-native operator" whose failure would indicate a protocol problem.** The diagnostic shows A fails even under correct protocol (prompt-only hooks, verified baseline, country pairs). A matches the printed equation but not the paper's verbal description or its behavioral result. The word "paper-native" for A needs qualification.

**Weakened:**

- **Kimi's F1 (ill-conditioned dual basis as leading cause of A/B failure).** The diagnostic shows A and B fail even with moderate cosines (well-conditioned 2×2 system). Ill-conditioning is not necessary for A/B failure — the failure is inherent to the two-coordinate exchange operator itself. F1 may still apply to the adjective pair specifically (untested), but it is no longer needed to explain A/B failure on country pairs.
- **Deepseek's QR-orthonormalized swap proposal.** Not directly tested, but since B (unit two-coordinate swap) also fails completely, and QR is a better-conditioned variant of the same swap family, it is weakened by association — though not ruled out.
- **The implicit hypothesis in all three reports that hook scope alone might rescue A.** The diagnostic uses prompt-only hooks and A still fails. Hook scope is insufficient to explain A's ineffectiveness. However, hook scope may still explain the old run's active damage (repetition, wrong-sign), distinct from mere ineffectiveness — the diagnostic's verbatim outputs show A + prompt-only hooks produces clean-but-unchanged output, not the pathological behavior of the old run.

## 4. Strongest surviving interpretation of why the old sycophancy run failed

The old run exhibited two distinct symptoms: (1) no positive effect toward the target behavior, and (2) active pathology (literal-token repetition, wrong-sign effects, fluency damage). The diagnostic, combined with the three reports, supports a **conjunction explanation** — no single factor is sufficient:

**The operator (A) was wrong — confirmed by diagnostic.** A cannot redirect answers even on the paper's own task domain with correct hooks and well-conditioned geometry. This explains symptom (1): no amount of protocol improvement would have made A produce the paper's effect.

**Generate-time hooks caused the pathology — supported by diagnostic's contrast.** The diagnostic shows A + prompt-only hooks on country pairs produces clean output (stays at " Paris." or " Ottawa." without repetition or damage). The old run's A + generate-time hooks produced repetition and damage. This difference is attributable to hook scope. The old run's symptom (2) — the actively harmful behavior — is most parsimoniously explained by the intervention being re-applied to each newly generated token, creating self-reinforcing feedback that writes both lens-token directions with large magnitude at each position.

**The fixed abrasive/flattering source was likely inactive or semantically wrong — untested but probable.** Even with C, if the source coordinate is near zero on unrelated prompts, the directed transfer is a near-no-op. If the lens vectors for ` abrasive` and ` flattering` encode surface token co-occurrence rather than behavioral concepts (GLM's concern), C would move activations in a direction that doesn't correspond to sycophancy behavior. The diagnostic does not test this.

**Inference, not observation:** the PI's updated probability table (45% hooks, 30% inactive source, 15% wrong operator) assigns the largest share to hooks. I read the diagnostic as showing that the wrong operator is a *necessary* condition for the old failure (since A cannot work even with correct hooks), while generate-time hooks and inactive source explain the *severity* of the symptoms beyond mere ineffectiveness. The 15% allocation to the operator seems low given the diagnostic's clarity, but the PI may be reasoning that a no-op operator wouldn't have produced the observed catastrophic behavior — the damage requires the hook-scope and/or source-activity problems in addition.

## 5. The next experiment that could still change the conclusion

**Run C (unit directed transfer) with prompt-only hooks on the sycophancy benchmark, after verifying per-prompt that the source concept is active in the lens (source coordinate exceeds a pre-registered threshold), and comparing against a mean-difference steering baseline (h + α(μ_flattering − μ_abrasive) from contrastive prompt sets).**

This is the experiment the diagnostic explicitly says has not been done ("does not yet show whether abrasive→flattering can steer sycophancy") and that all three reports identify as the next step after the country-pair gate.

**Why it could change the conclusion:**

- If C with verified active source steers sycophancy: the old run's failure was purely an implementation problem (wrong operator + wrong hook scope + unverified source), and the J-lens method generalizes beyond country facts. The conclusion becomes "the method works; the old code was broken."
- If C with verified active source does *not* steer sycophancy: the conclusion changes to "the J-lens method, even correctly implemented, cannot steer behavioral/style concepts on this model" — which is a substantive negative result about the method's scope, not just an implementation postmortem.
- If the mean-difference baseline also fails: the conclusion changes to "these style concepts are not linearly steerable in this model's residual stream by any method tested" — a stronger negative result about the model, not the method.

**Minority insight to preserve:** GLM's concern that the ` abrasive` lens vector may encode surface co-occurrence rather than behavioral semantics should be tested explicitly before the sycophancy run, by checking whether the abrasive/flattering cosine and norms are pathological (Kimi's E0 geometry check applied to the adjective pair specifically). If |cos| > 0.9, the two directions may be nearly indistinguishable in the lens, and C's "redirect" would be a near-no-op by construction.

## 6. Decision on which implementation for the corrected DEV rerun

**Decision: Use Candidate C (unit directed transfer) for the corrected DEV rerun.**

**Basis in observed evidence:**

- C is the only operator that reproduces the paper's behavioral result (6/6 on country pairs, all three bands, prompt-only hooks, α=1).
- A and B fail completely (0/6) despite exact coordinate-exchange verification and moderate cosine geometry.
- The paper's own prose description matches C, not the printed formula.
- The working replication used C and reported 97.2% on n=36 country trials.

**Uncertainty:**

- The diagnostic uses 2 country pairs × 3 bands = 6 trials per operator. This is a minimal sanity check, not a robust comparison. C's 6/6 could reflect favorable geometry specific to these pairs.
- C has not been tested on the sycophancy task. It may work for closed-vocabulary factual answers but not for open-ended behavioral style.
- The interaction between C and generate-time hooks has not been tested. The diagnostic uses prompt-only hooks for all three operators. If someone were to run C with generate-time hooks, it might also produce repetition/damage (the hooks problem is plausibly operator-independent).
- Deepseek's QR-orthonormalized swap remains untested and is theoretically distinct from both A/B and C. It could in principle redirect (unlike A/B) while preserving more two-coordinate structure (unlike C). But there is currently no evidence it outperforms C, and no behavioral data for it.

**Strongest objection:**

The diagnostic's sample is too small and too narrow to justify switching the entire implementation with high confidence. Two country pairs with single-token factual answers (Paris/Beijing, Ottawa/Cairo) are the paper's easiest category — the paper itself reports near-perfect success here for all its own attempts. The real test of whether C vs. A/B matters is on harder categories (months, animals, numbers) and on the sycophancy task. If someone objects: "you're switching to C based on 6 easy trials; run 10+ country pairs, the paper's other categories, and an explicit ablation of what happens if you just add α·d_t without subtracting the source component (a naive steering baseline) — if naive addition also works, the 'directed transfer' framing is doing less work than it appears, and the method reduces to standard activation steering along the target token's lens direction." This objection is legitimate and the suggested ablation is cheap. It does not reverse the decision (C is still the best-supported choice for the rerun), but it bounds the confidence: the decision is well-supported for country-fact tasks on this model, and uncertain for the actual sycophancy target task.
